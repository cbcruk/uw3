# Land raids and skirmishes, Maghreb / Iberian frontier c. 1450-1550: casualty check (round 2)

Scope: banditLethality 0.1, playerLethality 0.1 (on land), routRatio 0.5, beastLethality 0.06.
Builds on `docs/research/2026-10-09/combat.md` (round 1), which found nothing for land raids.

Legend: **[opened]** = I read the primary text in full-text OCR this session (archive.org djvu.txt);
**[snip]** = seen only in a search result or summary; **[mem]** = background knowledge, not re-checked.

Model under test (no luck term, deterministic): each 2-h round, losses_A = B × 0.1 and
losses_B = A × 0.1; a side routs when it has lost 50% of its starting strength.

Granada war and Castile (Pulgar) [opened]:

- Near Toro, 1476: Castilian and Portuguese men-at-arms fought for **four hours** until "quedáron muy pocos de los unos é de los otros que no fuesen muertos ó feridos". This was heavy cavalry choosing to fight it out. It is the only case found of a long, mutually bloody fight, and the sizes are not given.
- Granada, 1491: an ambush of a sortie. "mataron fasta quatrocientos Moros é mas de cien caballos, **sin que los Moros volviesen rienda á se defender**". This is the same pattern as cases 1, 4 and 8.

## Comparison with the model

The model, run deterministically on the cases with known sizes:

| Case                     | Model: rounds, P lost, M lost             | History                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1 (80 vs 110)            | 5 rounds; P 48 (60%), M 30 (27%), P routs | P 0, M 28; M broke at ~1%                                                                                    |
| 2 (32 vs 100)            | 2 rounds; P 20 (62%), M 5, P routs        | P 23 (72%), M "some"; P broke at ~19% and lost the rest in the pursuit. **The only case close to the model** |
| 4 (144 vs 900)           | 1 round; P 90 (62%), M 14, P routs        | P 1, M ~294 (≈30%)                                                                                           |
| 6 (300 vs 500)           | 4 rounds; P 184, M 91                     | P 0, M 0                                                                                                     |
| 7 (99 vs 107)            | 6 rounds; P 51, M 45, P routs             | P 0, M 107 (100%, no rout)                                                                                   |
| 10 (~30 engaged vs ~200) | 1 round; P 20 (67%), P routs              | P 0 men; M 111                                                                                               |

Expected loss per round = enemy × 0.1. With 100-900 enemies this means 10-90 dead per 2 hours. In
the harassment phase the record shows close to zero instead: cases 4 and 6 had hours of javelin
skirmish with 0-1 dead. The implied pre-contact lethality is below ~0.002 per enemy per round. Case 2,
where the pursuers kept up and ran down tired horses, gives about 6 killed by ~100 pursuers in roughly
one round, i.e. ~0.06. That is the highest rate found before a break.

### What the cases agree on (eight or more of the 13 opened cases)

1. **Casualties are almost one-sided.** The winner loses 0-1 men in cases 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12 and 13. Horses die far more often than riders (cases 10, 11 and 12).
2. **The break comes early and is triggered by events, not by attrition.** The triggers are an ambush appearing, the leader killed, or a formed charge hitting a disordered body. Breaks came at about 0% (case 4), ~1% (case 1), a few % (case 10) and ~19% (case 2, the leader dead). In no case did a mounted band stand to 50%.
3. **Most of the loser's losses happen after the break, in the pursuit**: 23 of 32 (case 2), ~30% of 900 (case 4), 111 (case 10). Two things decide the size of the pursuit loss:
   - **terrain:** a ford, cane brakes or hills turn a pursuit into a trap (cases 4, 5, 7 and 12);
   - **horse condition:** tired horses mean a large loss (cases 2 and 6).
4. **Foot caught by horse do not rout; they die or surrender** (case 7: 100%; case 12).
5. **Capture is a large share of "loss"**: 15 of 28, 44 of ~300, 47 of 107, and almost all of 24 in case 5. Raiders wanted captives (ransom), so "killed" overstates deaths.
6. Frontage matters on land too. Zurara: "the place was narrow, where only the front ranks could fight". Of 120 horse, 20 decided the fight.

### Verdict

- **Lethality 0.1 per enemy per 2-h round (bandits and the expedition's men): too lethal, and the wrong shape.**
  It is far too high for the skirmish phase: hours of contact with 0-1 dead, implying below 0.005. It is
  in the right range only for a pursuit of a broken side (case 2: ~0.06; case 4: ~294 lost to 144 pursuers
  in perhaps one round, ≈ 2 per pursuer, but that is mostly captures and drownings). No single value fits.
  Per the project rule this is a SHAPE problem (phase: skirmish → break → pursuit), not a table value.
- **Rout at 50%: too sticky for mounted raiders.** Mounted bands broke at 0-20%, usually at the moment of
  surprise or shock. This agrees with round 1's FM 105-5 direction (raiders ~0.8 remaining) and is now
  backed by period cases. Exception: foot caught in the open by horse do not rout at all (they are killed or captured).
- **Luck ±50%:** the real variance is bimodal (nothing happens, or one side is wrecked). A symmetric ±50%
  around a mean doesn't produce that.

## Wolves (in passing)

- Brown's note to Leo Africanus (Hakluyt ed. vol. 1, n. on Morocco fauna) [opened]: "**The wolf is not a Morocco mammal**, but the jackal (Canis aureus) and the fennec (C. cerdo) are, not to mention the hyena."
  Pory's "woolues" are jackals. Modern genetics reclassified the North African "jackal" as the African golden
  wolf (_Canis lupaster_, 2015), a ~10-15 kg animal with no record as a killer of adult men [mem].
- Leo's real large-predator danger in the Maghreb is **lions**, and they take stock and lone gatherers: "the most cruel and deuouring lions in all Africa, which there oftentimes doe seaze vpon them" (date gatherers); mules "often deuoured of lions" [opened].
- No wolf or lion attack on an armed caravan or party with fatalities was found. For the Maghreb or Sahara,
  "wolves vs. expedition" has no analogue. If a beast encounter is kept there, lion is the period animal;
  its threat is to stock and stragglers, not to armed men in a group. Grade stays `unverified`
  (candidate `not-applicable`, which needs a debt-baseline diff).

## Caravan raids (Leo) — qualitative only

- Arab tribes on caravan routes exact tolls; unpaid ones rob: "they will exact one fourth part of them for custome"; "unlesse he be safe conducted by their captaine, he is in danger to be robbed of altogither" [opened].
- Near Tunis: Arabs not in the king's pay "suddenly ... breake foorth, depriuing him of his goodes and life also: insomuch that now merchants dare not passe that way but with a garrison of safe-conduct" [opened].
- Leo himself was given an escort of "fiftie horsemen to accompanie and guard me from theeues" [opened].
- So the modal caravan–raider outcome was a toll, a safe-conduct fee or an escort deterrent, not a fight.
  This supports round 1's point that a zero-casualty payment outcome is missing from the model.
  No headcount-casualty raid on a caravan was found for c. 1450-1550.
