# Commodity prices c.1500, round 4: hides, raisins, cotton cloth, cork, gum arabic

> Copied from a research scratchpad on 2026-10-09. Quotes are from djvu/OCR text with light cleanup — check against page images before quoting elsewhere.

Research notes, 2026-10-09. Conventions as in `prices.md` to `prices-3.md`:
1 ducat = 375 mrs; 1 cruzado = 390 rs (1496-1516), 400 rs (from 1517); cruzado ≈ ducat.
Portuguese arroba = 14.69 kg, Portuguese quintal = 4 arrobas = 58.75 kg (Cape Verde used Portuguese weights).
d/t = ducats (or cruzados) per metric ton; base = producer price ÷ 0.8, or distant-market price ÷ 1.4.

## 1. Hides — current 45 → **keep 45, `unverified`**

New **[opened]** valuations from Santiago (Cape Verde), HGCV _Corpo Documental_ vol. 2, pp. 209-210 (ms. fl. 173-174).
They are customs-tithe assessments on hides exported from Ribeira Grande in Jan-Mar 1514:

- "Afonso Alvarez dezemou trinta e sete coiros dos xxx deu tres coiros / E os sete que ficaram foram avaliados em setecentos rs."
- "dizimou mais o sobredito nove coiros de bezeros que foram avaliados em quatrocentos e cinquoenta rs."
- "Mais dizimou quatro coiros que foram avaliados em quatrocentos rs." / "dous coiros que foram avaliados em duzentos rs." / "E os b [5] foram avaliados em b<sup>c</sup> [500] rs."
- "vynte peles cabruas. Foram avaliadas em seyscentos rs."

So: **cattle hide 100 rs each**, calf hide 50 rs, goat skin 30 rs (Santiago, 1514, official valuation at the producing port).

Conversion: 100 rs ÷ 390 = **0.256 cruzado per hide**.
The kg per hide is still **not in any source**. At my assumed 10-15 kg for a dry hide this is 17-26 d/t at the producer, so base = ÷0.8 = **21-32**. At 20 kg (green-salted) it would be 13 → base 16.

Compared with round 2: Portuguese hides in 1464-65 cost 0.61 cruzado each (Sequeira 2018), 2.4× the Cape Verde valuation. So island hides were cheap, or the customs valuation was low. Either way the game's 45 depends on an assumed weight and on which origin is meant (Maghreb/Ceuta in the game).

**Recommendation: keep 45 `unverified`.** Both price points are per piece. The 1514 valuation suggests 45 is at the high end if the game's hides are the cheap Atlantic kind. That is a reason to keep the range 20-45 in mind, not to change the value.

## 3. Cotton cloth (pano) — current 100 → **keep 100, `unverified`; new opened floor from raw cotton**

**Still no pano price before 1624.** But the Santiago records give the price of the **raw material**, so they bound cloth from below without assuming a pano weight. All [opened]:

Resold to the king at 300 → **13**.                                                                                                                   |

Reading:

- Seed (dirty) cotton is ~7-18 d/t at Santiago. Clean (ginned) cotton is ~85 at Santiago, or ~55 in the 1508 Madeira contract. That is a factor of ~5 by weight, as expected from ginning plus labour.
- The game's cotton cloth means hand-spun, hand-woven Santiago panos. **Cloth must cost more per kg than clean cotton**, so the opened floor is **≥ 55-87 d/t**.
- The current 100 is only 1.15-1.8× clean cotton. Spinning and weaving would normally add much more, so 100 is if anything low.
- Round 2's ≥100 guess (from Lisbon tow cloth) and round 3's ≥250 (from 1624 pano prices with an assumed weight) both stand as context.

**Grade: keep `unverified`.** No source prices a pano in the window. The floor is real and opened, but it is a bound, not a price. If a reviewer accepts a "floor" grade, **`qualitative` is possible only as "≥ 90"** (= clean cotton at Santiago, 87). It would not justify the specific value 100.

## 4. Cork — current 15 → **keep 15, `unverified`**

Nothing new.

- Godinho (opened, vol. II, malagueta chapter; the page number is not recoverable from the OCR) cites the 1472/1483 convention of the Flemish merchants in Lisbon. It lists "a cortiça" among Portuguese exports to the Low Countries.
- Muñoz Pomer (opened) notes that from 1470 foreign ships could not load "la sal y el corcho".

Both confirm that cork was an export good under royal control. Neither gives a price.

## 5. Gum arabic — current 38 → **keep 38, `unverified`**

Nothing priced. Opened sources only confirm the trade:

- Valentim Fernandes on Arguin: "goma arauiga" brought by the Moors of the mainland. He also says local people eat fresh gum and "nom na tem em estima".
- Godinho vol. I: gum from Arguin and the Sus "tão apreciada da indústria da seda do reino granadino".
- Ladero 1999 p. 82: gum arabic among Maghreb imports to Andalusia.

No Lisbon, Seville or Valencia price was found.

## 7. Caveats and next steps

- The Santiago numbers are **official valuations and accounting rates**, not open-market sales; the 1520 one is from an accusation of embezzlement. The "b<sup>c</sup>" = 500 reading follows the edition's convention (the same sign gives "b<sup>c</sup>xiiij" = 1514). I read the OCR, not the page image.
- The 1508 cotton = sugar parity uses round-1 Madeira sugar prices, so it inherits their grade.
- Hides: the only remaining route to a weight is a source that gives the same lot by count and by weight. The Valencia _dret portugués_ edition (full volume not online) or Sequeira 2023 might have one.
- Raisins: López de Coca 1997 (Genoa volume) and his _La tierra de Málaga_ (1977) are the targets. Neither is online.
- Gum and cork: no lead found that is both online and inside the window.
