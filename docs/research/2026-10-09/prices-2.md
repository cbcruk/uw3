# Commodity prices c.1500, round 2: the eight goods still unsourced

Research notes, 2026-10-09. This round follows the conventions in `docs/research/2026-10-09/prices.md`:

- 1 ducat = 375 mrs.
- 1 cruzado ≈ 1 ducat, worth 390 rs in 1496 and 400 rs from 1517.
- Castilian arroba = 11.5 kg and quintal = 46 kg. Portuguese arroba = 14.69 kg.
- d/t = ducats per metric ton.
- To get a base price: base = producer price ÷ 0.8, or distant-market price ÷ 1.4.

Two marks show how each source was read:

- **[opened]**: I read the PDF or the data file myself.
- **[snippet]**: I saw only a search-engine summary or a fragment.

## 1. Per-good findings

### Tuna (salted, Cádiz). Current base 20

| Evidence                                                           | Original                                                                                                                                                                         | → d/t                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cádiz almadrabas, 1486 **[opened]**                                | Ladero 1974-75 pp.102-103: _"En 1486, una 'cabeza de atún' valía 7,5 mrs., y una arroba de mojama, 130 mrs. (la arroba son 11,5 kg)."_ Ladero himself cannot interpret "cabeza". | 130 / 11.5 = 11.3 mrs/kg → ×1000/375 = **30 d/t** for mojama at the producer                                                     |
| Same, empty tuna barrel                                            | p.108: "Un barril para atún... 160 mrs." p.103: "la única referencia, tres arrobas por barril".                                                                                  | The barrel alone adds ≈ 160 / 34.5 kg ≈ **12 d/t** to the packed product.                                                        |
| Cádiz 1484 sale volume                                             | p.87 and p.103: tuna sold on one year's credit came to **1,623,842 mrs** (~4,500 ducats). | Medina Sidonia c.1540                                              | p.101, citing Pedro de Medina: 40,000-60,000 tuna a year gave the duke **20,000-30,000 ducats**, which is ≈ 0.5 ducat per fish.                                                  | At an assumed ~100 kg of salted yield per fish, that is ≈ 5 d/t. This is **net ducal income, not a price**, and dates from 1540. |
| Antwerp/Malines salt herring, 1500-1511 (cross-check) **[opened]** | GPIH/Van der Wee, Lent price per barrel of 66 L: average **1.26 g Ag/L**                                                                                                         | Assuming ~1 kg/L packed: 1.26 g Ag/kg ÷ 35.25 = **36 d/t** (40 at 0.9 kg/L). This is a regional staple near its own fishery.     |

Mojama is the premium product: dried loin, sold by weight. Barrelled salted tuna (_atún blanco_ / _badán_) was surely cheaper, but no number was found for it. The mojama price at the producer (30 d/t) and the herring price in its own region (~36 d/t) bracket salted fish at about **25-35 d/t at source**.

**Recommendation: base 30.** Mojama 30 ÷ 0.8 = 38 is an upper bound. Barrel tuna at roughly ⅔ of mojama gives ≈ 25. Grade **`qualitative`**. The single 1486 number comes from an opened source, but salted tuna itself is inferred from mojama.

Same-year ratio: Maghreb at Mazagán / Andalusian = 2,221 / 2,760 = **0.80**. Freight and average from Mazagán to Cádiz added 325 mrs/cahiz, about 15%.

All 1480s Cádiz levels are about 2× the Old Castile/Lisbon wheat of round 1 (5.6-5.7 d/t). Cádiz depended on grain imports, and 1486 was a dearth year.

**Recommendation: keep 5.** The evidence supports the _ratio_ Maghreb grain ≈ 0.8 × wheat, and with wheat base 6 that gives 4.8. Grade **`qualitative`**. The ratio comes from one year, and that year was a dearth year.

| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Portuguese and Galician ox hides bought at source by Pisan merchants, **1464-65** **[opened]** | Sequeira 2018 p.137: "6602 peças de couro de boi e 277 peles de cordeiro". p.138, Gráfico 1, average unit price of a generic hide: **Minho/Galiza 212, Coimbra 128, Évora 152 reais**. | Weight assumption (**mine, not sourced**)                                                      | A dried or dry-salted ox hide weighs ~10-15 kg; a green-salted one ~20 kg. I found no period figure.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | At **12 kg: 50 d/t**. At 15 kg: 40. At 20 kg: 30. Free on board, with the 14% logistics cost, 12 kg gives 57.                                                                                                      |

Portugal is the producer region here, so base = 40-50 ÷ 0.8 ≈ **50-63**. The Maghreb was a cheap source; Ladero p.92 lists _cueros_ among Cádiz's imports from Berbería. That argues for the lower end.

**Recommendation: base ≈ 45** (range 38-63 depending on hide weight). The current 25 is low by about 2×, unless hides are meant to be green and heavy. Grade **`qualitative`**: the price per piece comes from an opened archival-based study, but the kg per hide is my assumption and moves the result by ±30%. The date is 1464, but the price is gold-denominated through the cruzado.

### Raisins (Málaga). Current base 30

| Evidence                                                                 | Original                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | → d/t                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| England (Oxford, Cambridge, Sion, Finchale), 1450-1500 **[opened, OCR]** | Rogers vol. IV pp.668-669, on "raisins and figs" bought together by the "sort". Prices: 1453 8s 11d; 1460 8s 10d; 1465 14s; 1466-67 10s 6d; 1468 8s 6d; 1476 and 1482 12s; **1498 11s**. Average 10s 5½d. Rogers: _"agrees with the hypothesis that the 'sort' is 84 lbs., as this would be 1s. 6d. the dozen."_ Oxford 1490: frails at 4s each, three to the sort. An English penny of 1464-1526 held 0.719 g fine silver, so 1 ducat (35.25 g) = **49 d** at silver parity (at the market rate of ~54 d, results fall ~10%). 1.5 d/lb ÷ 0.4536 kg = 3.31 d/kg → **67 d/t** (1498: 71; 1490: 77). |
| Same market, sugar, for calibration **[opened]**                         | Rogers p.675: sugar per dozen lb was 2s 9½d in 1495, 6s in 1498, 3s in 1501, 2s 9d in 1503, 3s in 1505 and 4s in 1510. Typical ≈ **3s per dozen = 3 d/lb**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | **135 d/t**. As a distant market, 135 ÷ 1.4 = **96**. That matches the game's sugar base of 90, which validates the conversion.                                                                                                                                                         |

Raisins/sugar in the same market ≈ 1.55 / 3 = **0.52**, and the absolute value 67-77 ÷ 1.4 = **48-55**.

**Recommendation: base 50** (raisins ≈ 0.55 × sugar). The current 30 is about 40% low. Grade **`qualitative`**. The source is opened OCR, but the per-lb price rests on Rogers's own inference that a "sort" weighed 84 lb. "Raisins" in England c.1500 were mostly Spanish; Rogers later distinguishes "raisins of the sun, probably Malaga fruit, rather dearer". Lisbon dried figs in 1537 cost 512 rs per arroba of 14.69 kg, i.e. 87 d/t at the 1537 level (PWR **[opened]**), which is the same order.

| Evidence                                 | Original                                                                                                                                                                                                    | → d/t                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| England, 1566 and 1574 **[opened, OCR]** | Rogers vol. IV pp.632-633: _"Cork, used for floats, perhaps for other purposes too, costs 1½d. the pound in 1566 and 1574."_ The OCR reads "l\d."; elsewhere in this OCR "½" consistently comes out as "\". | After 1560 a penny held 0.480 g fine silver, so 1.5 d/lb = 0.72 g Ag/lb = 1.59 g Ag/kg → **45 d/t at 1570 prices**. Deflating to 1500: English prices ×~3.2 in pence × 0.667 silver per penny ≈ **×2.1 in silver**. That deflator is **from memory of Phelps Brown-Hopkins, not verified here**. **Recommendation: keep 15** (21 ÷ 1.4 ≈ 15). Grade **`unverified`**. This is one retail entry from 70 years too late, with an unverified deflator. It does at least confirm the order of magnitude: cork is a cheap bulk good, in the same range as wine.

PWR-Portugal has no cork series. Sequeira p.132 notes that from 1459-60 Portuguese exports were opened to foreigners _"à exceção da cortiça"_, i.e. cork was a reserved (monopoly) export. That supports cork as a Lisbon-controlled good but gives no price.

### Alum (no producer port; sold at 1.4× base). Current base 40

| Evidence                          | Original                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | → d/t                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tolfa, 1466 contract **[opened]** | de Roover 1948 p.47: the Societas Aluminum _"agreed to pay a royalty of two ducats to the Camera Apostolica for each cantaro of alum taken from the papal warehouses in Civitavecchia"_. n.47: _"The royalty was later reduced to one ducat per cantaro. The cantaro was a unit of weight of about 150 lbs. avoirdupois."_ The Camera also took ⅔ of extra profits. | 150 lb = 68 kg. **The royalty alone was 2 / 0.068 = 29 d/t** (1 ducat → 15 d/t), before mining cost, freight and the dealer's margin. |
| Low Countries, 1468 treaty        | p.50: the Duke of Burgundy "was to receive 6s. groat for each cantaro of papal alum imported".                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | That is a further levy of ~72 groats per cantaro, roughly 0.5-0.7 ducat → +8-10 d/t in Flanders.                                      |
| Phocaea, 15th c. (round 1)        | 100 cantars for 50 ducats                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | ≈ 10 d/t at the mine. **[snippet]**                                                                                                   |
| Mazarrón (Murcia), 1562 contract  | "16000 quintales anuales a 30 reales castellanos el quintal" (Simancas, cited in a Univ. Murcia paper). **[snippet]**: I could not open the PDF (digitum.um.es).                                                                                                                                                                                                    | 1,020 mrs/46 kg → 59 d/t at 1562 prices, ≈ 30 deflated to c.1500 (Hamilton level ~2×).                                                |

The papal cartel deliberately priced alum above cost. At Civitavecchia the floor is 29 (royalty) + mining, giving **≥ 35-40 d/t**. Delivered to Bruges or Seville it was then plausibly **50-70**, adding freight, the Burgundian levy and the Medici "one ducat per cantaro" margin from round 1 (+15). Since every port sells at 1.4× base, base ≈ 55-70 ÷ 1.4 = **40-50**.

**Recommendation: base 45** (40 is also defensible). Grade **`unverified`**. The royalty is opened and solid, but it is a floor, not a market price. I still have no market quotation c.1500 from an opened source. Delumeau 1962 and Zippel 1907 (_Archivio della R. Società Romana di Storia Patria_ XXX, 5-51, 389-461) are the sources to get.

### Cotton cloth (Cape Verde _panos_). Current base 32

There is **no price for panos c.1500.** Carreira (cited by Levy) dates the panos' use as money only to 1500-1520. The only priced panos in the Levy dissertation are from 1760: _"203 ordinários a 800 réis"_ per pano. That is unusable for 1500 in money terms, but it shows panos were priced **per piece**, at roughly a day-labourer's monthly wage.

Proxy: coarse cloth by weight at Iberian ports, **[opened]**:

| Cloth                                                       | Price                                                                 | Per kg (weight per metre is **my assumption**: 0.25-0.4 kg per running metre) |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Lisbon, 1521, _tow cloth_ (estopa, the coarsest flax cloth) | 15 rs/vara (vara 1.1 m) = 13.6 rs/m (PWR textiles)                    | 0.3 kg/m: 45 rs/kg → **114 d/t** (85-136)                                     |
| Lisbon, 1521-30, linen                                      | 45-60 rs/vara                                                         | **~400-550 d/t**                                                              |
| Cádiz, 1485, _cañamazo_ (canvas) for sacks                  | "la vara 25 mrs." (Ladero p.105). Castilian vara 0.836 m → 29.9 mrs/m | **~200-270 d/t**                                                              |

Even the coarsest woven cloth, priced by weight, is **≥ 100 d/t**. Woven cloth is a manufacture, an order of magnitude above raw staples. Indigo-striped panos traded as currency were probably not below coarse tow cloth.

**Recommendation: base ≥ 100, e.g. 120**, if the game's ton really means cloth weight. The current 32 looks 3-4× too low. Grade **`unverified`**. This is a proxy from a different fibre with an assumed weight per metre; nothing here is a pano price. If cheap cloth is wanted for gameplay, keep 32 and record it as authored.

### Gum arabic (Arguin). Current base 38

**Nothing found.** Searches in Portuguese, Spanish and English turned up only context. Gum was a minor Arguin export next to gold and slaves, and the European gum trade became large only after 1680 (Webb, _JAH_). The one apothecary hit, a Philip II royal botica list, gives "Goma Arábiga 14" with no unit; it is **[snippet]** and probably per onza at retail, so useless for wholesale.

**Recommendation: keep 38. Grade `unverified`.**

## 4. Caveats

- **The 1480s prices are pre-1497 maravedís.** Ladero converts the 1484 tuna sales at about 360 mrs per ducat ("1.623.842 mrs., unos 4.500 ducados"). Using 375 understates d/t by about 4%, which is negligible.
- **The 1486 grain and wheat prices are dearth prices.** Use the ratio, not the level.
- **The English (Rogers) prices are institutional purchases, mostly retail-ish and inland.** They are treated as a "distant market". Sugar in the same source calibrates to the game's 90 almost exactly, which is the main reason to trust the raisin conversion.
- **Three numbers depend on my assumptions:**
  - kg per hide;
  - kg per metre of cloth;
  - the 1570→1500 deflator for cork.

  Changing them moves the results by about ±30-50%, so none of these three should be graded `measured`.

- **Still unread and highest-value next:**
  - Hamilton 1934, Andalusian tables. It has raisins and possibly hides; every route to it was blocked.
  - Delumeau 1962 and Zippel 1907 for alum.
  - Carreira, _Panaria Cabo-Verdiano-Guineense_ (1968/1983), and Torrão in _História Geral de Cabo Verde_ II for panos.
  - Martín Gutiérrez & Ruiz Pilares on the Cádiz almadrabas for barrel-tuna prices.
