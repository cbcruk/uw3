# Round 3: ransom of one ordinary captured Moor, Iberian–Maghreb frontier c. 1450-1550

> Copied from a research scratchpad on 2026-10-09. Quotes are from djvu/OCR text with light cleanup — check against page images before quoting elsewhere.

Builds on `ransom-2.md` (Naples/Malta captive values) and `raids.md` (Arzila land-fight cases).
Legend: [opened] = I read the passage in the full text this session; [snippet] = seen only as a
search summary; [mem] = background knowledge, not re-verified.

Game question: a Portuguese party on horse breaks a Maghreb raiding band on land. What is one
captured _ordinary_ raider worth when ransomed through an intermediary (alfaqueque, "resgate")?

## Conversions used

- 1 ducado (Castile) = 375 maravedís (mrs).
- Portugal: 1 cruzado = 390 réis (1496-1516), 400 réis (1517-1555) (`prices.md`, PWR). Cruzado ≈
  Castilian ducat to ~1%, so **cruzados are taken 1:1 as ducats** below.
- **Dobra de Fez, 1516** [opened]: Rodrigues says 600 dobras de Fez fell short of 600 cruzados by
  "seiscentos meios vinténs, que notava seis mil reais", i.e. 10 réis per coin. So 1 dobra de Fez ≈
  cruzado − 10 rs ≈ 380-390 rs ≈ **0.97 ducat**.
- **Dobla haçén (zahén/jayén), Málaga 1490s** [opened, secondary]: "doblas hacenes, razonadas a 450
  maravedíes la pieza; la dobla castellana normal equivalía a 365 maravedíes" (González Arévalo 2005,
  p. 352 n. 22). So 1 dobla haçén = 450/375 = **1.2 ducats**.
- Price level. Rodrigues stresses more than once that prices in his own day (he wrote c. 1550s-60s)
  were far above those of the 1510s. Examples: horses bought "por pouco mais de cem cruzados, valendo
  no tempo em que estamos muitos centos" (I, p. ~125). The Arzila figures below are given in the
  money of the year of the event.

## 1. Portuguese Morocco: Bernardo Rodrigues, _Anais de Arzila_ [opened]

Ed. David Lopes (Lisboa 1915-20), vol. I https://archive.org/details/anaisdearzilacrn01rodr , vol. II
https://archive.org/details/anaisdearzilacr02rodruoft (djvu OCR, grepped for "resgat", "alfaqueque",
"dobras", "cruzados", "vendid", then each hit read in context). Page numbers come from the OCR page
marks and are approximate to ±1.

The practice the chronicle shows. Captured Moors were **first sold at auction** ("o conde mandou vender
os cinco mouros em leilão, como sempre foi costume", I ch. XXI, p. 176). The proceeds went to the
cavalgada's shares. The buyer then **held the man for ransom**, negotiated through an alfaqueque or
a letter left on the beach. Some captives were instead exchanged one-for-one for Christians.
The garrison's on-the-spot cash was therefore the **auction (sale) price**. The ransom was realized
later by whoever bought the man.

### A. Ordinary man vs notable, 1516 (I, ch. XXI, pp. 176-177) [opened]

Five "mouros honrados" were taken during the 1516 siege. One of them, Bençude (born in the Lisbon
Mouraria, with a house in Fez), was auctioned to Fernão Caldeira "por setenta ou oitenta mil reais".
Bençude, put to the hand-mill in irons, objects:

> "não deu tanto por mim pêra me ter a úa mó e lhe moer um alqueire de trigo, nem lhe alimpar um
> cavalo, que iso faz **um bárbaro que custa dez ou doze mil reais**, mas a mim não me comprou senão
> pêra lhe dobrar seu dinheiro e me resgatar"

Caldeira asks 600 cruzados. Within three days a Moor under a flag of truce brings "seiscentas dobras
de Fez". On the same visit he ransoms the honrado bought by the countess "por mil e dozentos
cruzados", and three other companions (sums not given).

| Item (1516)                                                | Original           | Ducats (390 rs/cz) | Kind                     |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------ | ------------------------ |
| "um bárbaro" (common rustic captive), sale as a work slave | 10,000-12,000 réis | **26-31**          | sale price               |
| Bençude, honrado, auction price                            | 70,000-80,000 réis | 180-205            | sale price (speculative) |
| Bençude, ransom                                            | 600 dobras de Fez  | ~585               | ransom, cash             |
| Countess's honrado, ransom                                 | 1,200 cruzados     | ~1,200             | ransom                   |

Bençude's ransom was about 3 times what he sold for. That matches the Naples ratio in `ransom-2.md`
("mediamente era il triplo"). "dobrar seu dinheiro" was the buyer's stated aim.

### B. An ordinary captive's ransom, 1525 (II, ch. VII, pp. ~20-23) [opened]

The author's own captive was a Moor who "fora guarda da ponte d'Alcacere Quebir", an ordinary
bridge sentry, not a notable. He turned guide, took two foot guards at the bridge, and was baptized,
so his ransom was lost. The captain compensated the author:

> "dos dous mouros me deu um, o que eu quis escolher, e, por não valer tanto como o meu, o capitão me
> rogou o tomase e que ele ficava de refazer-me em presas que o mouro fizese **até cem cruzados, que
> por ele me dávão de resgate**"

So **100 cruzados (~100 ducats at 400 rs) had been offered as ransom for an ordinary sentry**. The
foot guards taken at the bridge were worth less than him. Later (II, p. ~41) the author counts the loss
as "outros cento que polo mourisco o capitão ficou de me dar".

### C. Other Arzila values (context) [opened]

- Wife and child of a Moorish convert, held by Caldeira, bought out "por cento e cincoenta cruzados"
  (II, pp. ~10-11). A different woman was ransomed by Mulei Abrahem for 150 cruzados (II, p. 13).
- "um mourinho, que valia outros tantos ou mais", i.e. a Moorish boy worth ≥40 cruzados, 1520s
  (II, p. 26). Sale value, given in part payment for a horse.
- The page of the alcaide Mafote "saio por quatrocentos cruzados", c. 1530 (II, p. ~78). This is a
  household retainer of a notable.
- A Moor taken by eight spies was sold to two merchants. He feigned illness, "parecendo-lhe que, sendo
  mao servidor, o darião por pouco resgate" (II, p. ~78). Ransom was negotiable down for a poor servant.
- The Christian side, for comparison, in the author's later day: "sai um pobre cativo por dozentos
  cruzados" (I, ch. X, p. 145).
- The 1509 Algarrafa captives (15 riders, cf. `raids.md` case 1): "Fôrão estes mouros vendidos asaz
  barato, ainda que neles avia mouros honrados e criados do alcaide" (I, p. 32). No sum is given.

## 2. Castile–Granada / Alborán frontier

### D. Lezcano's 24 Moors, Vélez de la Gomera raid, 1498 [opened, secondary citing notarial archive]

Raúl González Arévalo, "Cautivos moros y judíos en Málaga en tiempo de los Reyes Católicos", _Baetica_
27 (2005), pp. 345-362, cuadro 1, p. 352 (A.H.P.M. leg. 2, fols. 293v-300, 9-VII-1498).
PDF: https://www.revistas.uma.es/index.php/baetica/es/article/download/324/275/1167

Juan de Lezcano, captain of the royal fleet, raided near Vélez de la Gomera and brought back 24 Moors.
Instead of the customary public auction he agreed an individual ransom with each man, payable "por lo
general en un plazo de seis meses". The author reads this as the better deal: "cantidad altísima que
explicaría que Lezcano prefiriera concertar el rescate con cada uno de los cautivos antes que venderlos
como esclavos en almoneda pública a precio de mercado". Professions and wealth are not recorded.

Ransoms in doblas haçenes (1.2 ducats each):

- 60, 70, 75, 80, 80, 80, 80, 90, 90 (9 men)
- 100 × 7
- 180, 180, 190, 200, 250, 250, 300, 600

The total is 3,550 doblas. The **median is 100 doblas = 45,000 mrs = 120 ducats**. The lower two
thirds (16 men) paid 60-100 doblas = **72-120 ducats**. The top (600 doblas = 720 ducats) is
plainly a notable. These are men taken in a raid on the Maghreb coast, a close match in kind to a
band's rank and file. But the source is a sea raid by Castilians, not a land fight by Portuguese.

### E. Málaga 1487, collective ransom [opened]

Andrés Bernáldez, _Historia de los Reyes Católicos_ (Sevilla 1870), vol. I, ch. LXXXVI-LXXXVII,
pp. 253-256: https://archive.org/details/historiadelosrey00bern

> "el concierto del rescate fué de esta manera: Que le dieran por todos los que aquel dia se hallaron
> vivos, así chicos como grandes, á **treinta doblas jayenes por cada uno varones é mujeres, chicos é
> grandes**"

Their goods were taken on account, and the balance was due in eight months. Then: "no pudieron cumplir
el rescate, y quedaron todos cautivos del Rey é de la Reina" (p. 256). The 450 Jews were ransomed by
Abraham Señor for "veinte mil doblas jayenes", about 44 doblas per head.

30 doblas haçenes = 13,500 mrs (González Arévalo gives 13,400) = **36 ducats per head, every age and
sex**. This is a capitulation head-tax on a whole city that it then failed to pay. It is not an
individual soldier's ransom. Individual Málaga ransoms of 1497-99 (women, a "moro mancebo") were
10,000-17,000 mrs = **27-45 ducats** (González Arévalo p. 348). Pulgar (opened, same episode) gives only
the three-way division of the captives, with no price.

## 3. Ceuta / Alcácer Ceguer: Zurara, _Crónica do Conde D. Duarte de Meneses_ [opened]

_Collecção de livros inéditos_ III (1793), https://archive.org/details/collecadeliv03corruoft

- Son of "aquelle grande Xeque ... Abdela Laroz": "se dar por aquelle preso **duas mil dobras, e
  mais três cavallos sellados**" (ch. after LXII, p. 173-174). This is a notable's ransom, cash plus
  horses. The dobra of the 1460s is not converted here (it was probably a gold dobra of the order of
  1 ducat or more [mem]), so this is a 2,000+ ducat notable.
- Several exchanges of Moors for Christians: "foi feito hum resgate de certos Mouros por outros
  Christãos" (p. ~195); the count sent a fusta to Tangier "a resgatar hum seu Mouro por hum Christaõ".
  These are exchanges in kind, with no price.
- No price for an ordinary Moor found.

## Synthesis

| Quantity                                          | Value (ducats c. 1500)    | Basis                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ordinary captive, **sale** as slave (on the spot) | **~26-36**                | Arzila 1516 "bárbaro"; Málaga 1487 head-rate                      |
| Ordinary man, **ransom**                          | **~70-120, central ~100** | Arzila 1525 sentry (100 cz); Lezcano 1498 median 100 doblas = 120 |
| Retainer of a notable                             | ~400                      | Arzila, Mafote's page                                             |
| Notable (honrado, alcaide's kin, xeque's son)     | ~600-2,000+               | Bençude, countess's Moor, Laroz's son                             |
| Ransom / sale ratio                               | ~3×                       | Bençude; matches Naples (`ransom-2.md`)                           |

Distinctions that matter for the game:

1. **Ransom vs sale.** The garrison sold its captives at auction, at roughly 25-35 ducats for a
   common man. Ransom was about 3 times that, but it took weeks to months and an intermediary. It was
   also paid in instalments (Lezcano: six months), sometimes in cloth or goods. "On the spot" cash is
   the sale price. Ransom is the higher, delayed price.
2. **Cash vs goods.** Ransoms were mostly cash (cruzados, dobras de Fez), but goods appear: cloth
   (Caldeira's own ransom was paid in "cincoenta e cinco peças de pano azul de Ingraterra"), horses
   (Laroz), silk, soap, oil and butter as gifts (Bençude). Exchange one-for-one against Christian
   captives was common.
3. **Ordinary vs notable.** About a 6-20× spread. The modal figure of ~100 applies only to the rank
   and file.

## Recommendation

**~100 ducats per ordinary captured raider, ransomed. Grade `qualitative`.**

The grade rests on two [opened] figures in this sense. The first is Rodrigues's 1525 bridge sentry,
for whom "cem cruzados ... por ele me dávão de resgate" (Arzila, a Portuguese frontier garrison, an
ordinary man). The second is Lezcano's 1498 notarial ransoms, with a median of 100 doblas haçenes =
120 ducats for 24 Maghreb men (secondary article quoting the archive).

Caveats to carry in the table note:

- Range 70-120. The low end is the 1498 lower tail (60-80 doblas). Some foot captives are explicitly
  "worth less" than the 100-cruzado sentry.
- If the game pays **immediately on the field**, the historically faithful value is the **sale
  price, ~30 ducats** (Arzila 1516, opened, same grade). 100 is the delayed ransom through an
  alfaqueque. Pick one meaning and state it.
- Notables must not be averaged in. If a band leader can be captured, a separate value of ~500+ fits.
- The source counts are thin: one Arzila case and one 24-man list. Do not tighten beyond ±30%.
