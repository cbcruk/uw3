## Conversions used

- 1 ducado (Castile) = 375 maravedís (money of account), as in round 1.
- Neapolitan ducat (ducato di regno, 10 carlini), 1570s-80s: ~0.8 Castilian ducado [mem; plausible
  range 0.75-0.9, not verified this session]. Below, "N. ducats" means Neapolitan ducats.
- Malta scudo (écu) = 12 tarì; Venetian sequin = 16 tarì, so 1 sequin = 1.33 scudi (rate stated in the
  Malta source below, 1591). Treating the sequin as ~1 gold ducat ≈ 1 Castilian ducado [mem, approximate],
  1 Malta scudo ≈ 0.75 ducado.

## 1. plunderGold = 10 ducats per raider of the defeated band

### A. Use-value of captured Muslims on the Naples galleys, 1575 and 1585 [opened]

Filioli Uranio, F., "Quién tiene esclavo tiene oro: prezzo e valore degli schiavi a Napoli alla fine del
XVI secolo", in a 2020 CHAM (NOVA FCSH, Lisbon) volume (_Culturas e dinâmicas..._, pp. 159-171).
PDF: https://livros.fcsh.unl.pt/cham/catalog/download/154/542/1723?inline=1 (text extracted and read).
The chapter summarizes Aymard 1973 and Vincent 2011, which draw on AGS, Estado, Libro 42.

- **1575 appraisal of 940 galley slaves** (from Aymard 1973, p. 58): "100 ducati per gli adulti da 25 a 30
  anni, che erano anche sani e di giusta taglia". Around 40 years old, 60-70 ducats. A 56-year-old sick
  man, 40 ducats.
- **1585 asiento of 26 Neapolitan galleys, 657 slaves** (from Vincent 2011, p. 843). Mean valuation by
  age: 20-29 y **103.1**, 30-39 **98.7**, 40-49 **91.8**, 50-59 **72.2**, 60+ **46.8** N. ducats.
  Extremes: "uno schiavo di 22 anni valutato 125 ducati e ... uno di 60 anni valutato 15 ducati".
  Of the 657 slaves, 214 came from Asia Minor, 121 from the Maghreb and 52 from Istanbul. These are
  the men corsair crews were drawn from.
- **Ransom ("exchange value") vs use-value.** For 23 of the slaves a ransom price is also recorded. The
  ransom was "almeno il doppio (200 ducati contro 100), poteva arrivare a otto volte di più (600 ducati
  contro 70) e mediamente era il triplo". Example: Odoverdi, a 44-year-old Turk, was valued at 60 and
  ransomable "per 300 ducati".
- Valuations in the asiento covered everything: "dagli scafi, al cordame, alle munizioni venne
  rendicontato". The hull figures are not quoted in the chapter.

Arithmetic, taking a fighting-age corsair at roughly 90-100 N. ducats:

- 90-100 N. ducats × 0.8 ≈ **72-80 ducados per man as a galley slave** (use-value, the price a state or
  asentista would pay).
- As ransom: about 3 × that, so roughly **200-250 ducados**. Ransom was only realizable for a minority:
  23 of 657 had a recorded ransom.

### B. Malta, late 16th century: ransoms of Muslim slaves [opened]

Geneanum (Malta), "Daily Life of Slavery in Malta in the Sixteenth Century", based on Maltese notarial
and Inquisition records:
https://en.geneanum.com/malta/documents/history/lesclavage-au-quotidien-a-malte-au-xvie-siecle.html.
This is a secondary popular article that cites archives. I read it through a fetch summary.

- Average ransom at the end of the century "fluctuated between approximately 120 and 180 écus".
- 1591: 18 slaves of the Order had an average ransom of "120 sequins, or 160 écus". A raïs (corsair
  captain) was ransomed for "400 sequins (or 534 écus)".
- 1597: renting out an Order slave cost 18 tarì per month (~1.5 scudi).

Converted at 1 scudo ≈ 0.75 ducado, the average ransom is **~90-135 ducados**, and a corsair captain
~400 ducados. This is consistent with A: ransom sits above galley use-value.

### C. Hull of a captured fusta/galliot

No figure found. The 1585 Libro 42 does value hulls, but the opened chapter does not quote them.
Spanish galley-construction cost studies (Aguilera López on the Barcelona Atarazanas) were found but not
opened.

### Bearing on plunderGold = 10

The sourced quantity is **~70-80 ducados per captured fighting-age man** as a galley slave (Naples 1575
and 1585, opened). The ransom value is higher, but most captives never realized it. That is 7-8 × the
current 10.

Two readings:

1. **If plunder excludes prisoners** (coin, arms, clothing taken from the beaten band): 10 has no source.
   Nothing found prices a corsair's purse or kit. The grade stays `unverified`.
2. **If plunder is meant to include selling captured raiders** (the main real-world gain, "quién tiene
   moro tiene oro"), the sourced value is ~75 ducados per _captured_ man. In a won fight not every
   raider is taken alive. For example, half captured gives ~35-40 ducados per raider of the band. The
   capture fraction is a design assumption, not a sourced value.

**Recommendation.** Keep 10 and grade it `unverified` unless the game decides that captured raiders are
sold. In that case set ~35-40 per raider, with an explicit capture-fraction assumption, and grade it
`qualitative`. That grade rests on the opened Naples valuations, which match in period and region and
measure the right kind of asset. Note that the prisoners from a Barbary galliot's _rowing bench_ were
mostly Christian captives who were freed, not sold. Only the Turkish/Moorish fighting crew carried
slave value. That fits a "per raider" unit.

## 2. tributeGold = 15 ducats per corsair (pay to be let go)

### What was looked for and not found

- A merchant ship paying a corsair band a sum on the spot to be released ("composición", "rescate de la
  nave", "rançon" of a ship), c. 1450-1600, with a sum and the attacker's crew size. Searched in Spanish,
  French, Italian and English, covering Barbary, Uskok, Hospitaller and French/English privateers.
  **No case with a sum was found.**
- The French "billet de rançon" (ransom bill) is well documented, but every sum found was from the
  17th-18th century. Examples: a 1748 Guernsey bill for £1000 and a 1798 bill for $2000 [snippet]. No
  16th-century sum surfaced.
- Andrews, _Elizabethan Privateering_ (prize values 1589-91) and Tenenti, _Piracy and the Decline of
  Venice_ exist on archive.org only as lending copies (bwb_*), with no full text. They were not read.

### What was found (qualitative only)

- **Immediate offshore ransom ("alafia") is documented as a practice** [opened]. Source: Isabella
  Lambert, "Corsairs' activity and contracts", _Journal of Maritime Research_ XX(3), 2023, pp. 110-118:
  https://www.jmr.unican.es/jmr/article/view/743. Captives "could either be released immediately on the
  boat ... thanks to the process of the 'Alafia' and the payment of a verbally agreed ransom". Corsairs
  "anchored their ship off the French or Spanish coasts and conducted their negotiations from their
  boats". The article stresses that these deals left little written trace: "The traces left by this type
  of negotiation and release were much more discreet". It gives **no sums** for alafia. Its sums are
  17th-century Tunis captive ransoms: "from one hundred to five hundred piastres for captives of average
  value", plus gate fees of 33-35 piastres. That is the per-captive quantity already covered in round 1.
- So the _mechanic_ (pay corsairs on the spot to be let go) has a sourced historical analogue. It
  applies to people more clearly than to ships, but no sourced _price_ was found. Part of the reason is
  that these deals were by nature unwritten.

### Cross-check from this round's numbers (design reasoning, not a source)

A corsair band that takes a merchantman gains its crew as captives. The opened Naples and Malta figures
put a captive at ~75 ducados use-value and ~100-250 ducados as ransom. The round-1 Mercedarian average
was ~150 ducados. With a merchant crew of ~15-20, capture is worth ~1,500-3,000+ ducados to the
corsairs, before ship and cargo.

The current tribute of 15 ducats × attackers gives 1,350 ducados for a 90-man galliot crew, which is
below the capture value. That ordering is sensible: a rational corsair accepts tribute only when
fighting is costly. But this is a consistency check, not a sourced value.

**Recommendation.** Keep 15, grade `unverified`. The `qualitative` evidence (Lambert 2023) supports the
existence of on-the-spot ransom, not its size, so it does not meet the bar. If a sourced anchor is
wanted, the defensible quantity is still per _own crew member_, as round 1 said: about 100-150
ducados × a fraction under 1. That would be a model change.
